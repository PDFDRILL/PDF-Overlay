## The goal, as the coordinator reads it (to be checked against the chat)
A large matrix with many empty or sparse cells is open white space to
MathPix: columns and rows lose their alignment, and the LaTeX comes back
with cells merged or shifted. PDF-Overlay puts a small mark (an
underscore, a point) into each open cell of such a matrix before the page
goes to MathPix, so every cell has ink and the grid is read whole. The
marks are then known placeholders: they are taken out of the returned
LaTeX by position, and what remains is compared with the page.

## Relations already in the group (coordinator)
- Where the open cells are: GLYPHDRILL's white space (T-0053): A1 white
  blobs and A3 gutters; the owner's hard case there was "open white space
  inside a matrix", and gutters record their supported rows so a matrix
  column gap is told from a page column. T-0059's graph gives the cells as
  white nodes whose ink neighbours stay inside the matrix.
- Where the matrix is: born-digital, pdf2mmd2 / pdfdrill's stream reader
  (T-0045) gives the glyph positions; a scan, inkdrill's blobs and the
  layout detector.
- Closing the loop: inkdrill's ink chain (pdfdrill `inkreport`,
  `residuals`, `refine`) measures MathPix's LaTeX rendered against the
  scan; an overlay is accepted only when that distance falls. The marks
  must be invisible to that measure or removed before it.
- MathPix is the paid lane (pdfdrill `mathpix`); no audit spends a key
  without the owner's word.
- After LaTeX: Lean 4 as the format for reasoning (owner, above);
  `contracts/workspace.md` already makes a Lean theorem the check of a
  proof obligation (TOP-13), and pdfdrill has `lean`.

## Long-term aim (owner, above)
OCR ↔ pdf2mmd ↔ LaTeX closed through the blobs, so that later models can
be trained to read PDFs with tables, images and mathematics; LaTeX is not
the last format, Lean 4 a candidate.

## Decisions (owner, 2026-10-22)
"TOP-20 repo cann start public but will go private later, start with born
digital, but actual solution works with ghostscript and does not depend on
the source!"
- Visibility: public at the start, private later.
- First slice: born-digital PDFs.
- The overlay is applied through Ghostscript to the PDF itself; it does
  not depend on the LaTeX source. So the source (arXiv e-print) is at most
  a check of the result, never the input, and the marks are placed from
  what the PDF and its rendering show (glyph positions from the stream
  reader, white space from the page image).
